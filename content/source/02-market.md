# UtopiaHire — Market & Audience

UtopiaHire serves job seekers, with a primary focus on Tunisia and secondary reach into French-speaking and international markets.

## Audience

- Tunisian graduates and professionals preparing their first international applications.
- French-speaking job seekers who want ATS-friendly CVs.
- Career switchers who need their resume rewritten for a new direction.
- Job seekers applying to many roles at once who need a tracking system (agents, applications, stats) instead of a spreadsheet.
- Candidates facing rejection who want to understand *why* their CV underperforms, not just "try again".

## Market context & pain points

- Most free CV tools produce either a generic one-page PDF or a score with no actionable reasoning. Neither helps a candidate decide what to actually change.
- Recruiters and ATS filters discard CVs for structural reasons (missing keywords, poor section order, inconsistent dates) that candidates never see.
- Tunisian job seekers often apply internationally, which makes language switching (French/English) and format differences a real, concrete problem.
- Job search is a numbers game, but the tools candidates use treat every application as a one-off instead of a pipeline with a funnel.

## Product-market fit notes

- The diagnosis-first flow (upload → findings → rewrite → match) maps directly to the real candidate journey, unlike a one-shot "resume score".
- Subscription pricing fits a job-search phase rather than a long-term tool: during an active search, candidates pay for speed; between searches they pause.
- A bilingual product is not a translation layer here — French and English CVs follow different conventions and must be built, not machine-translated.

## Evidence & signals the product tracks

- CV diagnosis completion rate (did the user act on findings?).
- Section rewrite usage per tone (which tone gets used in real applications).
- Job match score distribution (are matches landing in a useful range?).
- Application funnel: saved → applied → interview → rejected → accepted.
